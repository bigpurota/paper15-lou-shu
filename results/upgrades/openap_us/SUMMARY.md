## Interpretation (of the numbers above)

- `Illiquidity` premium in 1964–2012: 0.32% (t 2.78, significant at 5% but does not clear HLZ); after 2013: -0.14% (t -1.47, not significant); difference -0.47 (t -3.08, significant at 5%, borderline at HLZ (see the NW-lag sensitivity below)). For `DolVol` the difference is -0.73 (t -3.12, significant at 5%, borderline at HLZ (see the NW-lag sensitivity below)).
- Alpha of `Illiquidity` on the `DolVol` L-S: 0.05% (t 0.63, not significant) in 1964–2012 and -0.14% (t -1.40, not significant) after 2013. In the Lou–Shu sample the significant Amihud premium becomes insignificant after controlling for the volume factor, qualitatively as in the paper's Table A.1.
- Caveat to this conclusion: the `Illiquidity` alpha on FF3+UMD alone is also not significant (0.14%, t 1.60), so in the OpenAP version size/value/momentum already remove the premium, and the contribution of the volume factor itself is harder to isolate than in the paper.
- After 2013 there is no premium even without factors, so the 'volume or price' question is not identified in this subperiod.
- The FF3+UMD alpha of `Illiquidity` is 0.14% (t 1.60, not significant) in 1964–2012 and 0.16% (t 1.66, not significant) after 2013, and its SMB loading moves from 0.70 (t 14.93) to 0.24 (t 4.46). The alpha is insignificant in both periods, so there was no significant abnormal premium that could vanish; the fall of the raw L-S is consistent with lower size (SMB) exposure. Neither the alpha difference nor this SMB attribution is tested formally.
- Sensitivity of the t of the 'after − before' difference to the number of NW lags (the main specification, 6, follows the paper and the base `config.yaml`; the others are checks only, `decay_nw_sensitivity.csv`): `Illiquidity`: 0 lags t -2.58, 3 lags t -3.00, 6 lags t -3.08, 12 lags t -2.97; `DolVol`: 0 lags t -2.64, 3 lags t -3.00, 6 lags t -3.12, 12 lags t -3.11. Clearing the HLZ bar is not robust to the choice of lags: the decay is significant at 1.96 and borderline at HLZ.

## Caveats

- This is not our own computation on CRSP but ready-made OpenAP portfolios (a cross-check, not a substitute for our own code).
- The OpenAP construction differs from the paper (Lou–Shu: NYSE/AMEX, monthly measure A at t−2, EW quintiles; the IMLA_C factor uses A_C terciles): OpenAP has its own universe, filters, windows and quantiles (lines below and `signal_meta.csv`); the volume factor here is the DolVol L-S, not IMLA_C; FF3+UMD are the VW French factors.
- The post-2013 subperiod is short; the 'after − before' difference tests for decay, not for zero.
- The 2013 break is the end of the Lou–Shu sample, not the publication date of the signals themselves (`DolVol`: Brennan, Chordia, Subra 1998, `Illiquidity`: Amihud 2002); this is a test 'outside the Lou–Shu sample', not a test of publication decay in the McLean–Pontiff (2016) sense: part of 1964–2012 already lies after publication.
- The two L-S series use different universes: OpenAP `Illiquidity` is NYSE only (exchcd==1) with price > 5 (filter from SignalDoc), while `DolVol` uses all CRSP stocks without a filter, including NASDAQ, which Lou–Shu exclude precisely because of its inflated volume. The Table A.1 regression here is therefore an NYSE portfolio on a whole-market factor.
- Timing of `DolVol` (from the OpenAP code): log dollar volume of a single month, t−3 (signal = volume at l2, applied to the next month's return).
- Timing of `Illiquidity` (from the OpenAP code): 12-month average, formed in June and held for 12 months (lag 1–12 months, no skip month).
- Construction of `DolVol` per SignalDoc: weights EW; rebalanced every 1 months; filter n/a; L-S quantile 0.2 (OpenAP default, quintiles), breakpoints over all stocks after the filter; definition: Log of two-month lagged trading volume (vol) times two-month lagged price (prc).
- Construction of `Illiquidity` per SignalDoc: weights EW; rebalanced every 12 months; filter abs(prc)>5, exchcd==1; L-S quantile 0.2 (OpenAP default, quintiles), breakpoints over all stocks after the filter; definition: Past twelve month average of: daily return (abs(ret)) divided by turnover((abs(prc)*vol)
