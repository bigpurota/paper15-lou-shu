# Deviations from the paper (Lou & Shu 2017)

Each deviation lists what the paper does, what we do, why, and how this may affect the results.

| # | Paper | Us | Reason | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | NYSE/AMEX, CRSP, 1964–2012 | Binance spot, USDT pairs, returns 2019-01 to 2026-09 | No CRSP access; task, section 3 | Fewer months (≈93 vs 588) and fewer assets, so less power; a different market (24/7, retail, wash trading) |
| D2 | Turnover measures `AT`, `AT_C`, size `S`, `ln(ME)` control | None | Binance has no supply / market capitalisation data | Table 2 and the models with `AT` and size are not replicated; FM has no size control |
| D3 | `B/M` control | None | Coins have no book value | The control is dropped |
| D4 | FF3-adjusted returns, betas over [t−60, t−1], minimum 24 | CMKT-adjusted (EW market of the universe), betas over [t−36, t−1], minimum 12 | No SMB/HML for crypto; coins have short histories | Weaker risk adjustment; raw returns are reported as robustness |
| D5 | `r − r_f` | `r_f = 0` | The risk-free rate is the same for all coins in a month, so it does not affect FM slopes or H–L | Shifts only quintile levels and intercepts |
| D6 | 4-factor alpha (MKT, SMB, HML, UMD) | Alpha on CMKT + CMOM (tercile H–L on Ret[−12,−2]) | No size or B/M | The alpha is comparable to CAPM+MOM, not to Carhart |
| D7 | IVOL: residuals of a daily FF3 regression over the past year | Residuals of a daily regression on the daily EW CMKT (mean over all valid coin-days, no volume filter), 12 months, window ending at t−2, ≥120 days | No daily SMB/HML; the window ends at t−2 so that it uses no information from after the measure | Cruder volatility control |
| D8 | Universe: share codes 10/11 | Stablecoins, fiat, gold, wrapped/staked tokens and leveraged tokens excluded; at least $10k mean daily volume | These are not the "common shares" of the crypto market | The volume filter cuts the most illiquid tail, which is conservative for the premium |
| D9 | CRSP daily return including dividends | Close-to-close over UTC days; a day is valid if the previous day's close exists | Binance klines | Days missing because of maintenance are excluded, not spliced |
| D10 | CRSP (the paper does not describe how it treats delistings; the standard practice is CRSP delisting returns) | Monthly return from the last available close | Binance has no delisting returns; a coin can simply be removed | Losses at delisting may be understated (estimate: small effect, but its direction favours illiquid coins) |
| D11 | January seasonality test of the liquidity explanation (Section 4.1, Table 11: is the volume premium seasonal in January like the liquidity premium?) | January only as a placebo (`T7_january.csv`) | Our sample has only 7 Januaries, so the test has very little power. | D13 | — | Simulations: calendar months have 28–31 days, so `simulation.days_per_month` from the config is not used; in world P λ = exp(l)·DVol^(−0.5), and the priced part is the part of λ beyond the square-root law (independent of volume) | Otherwise the daily volatility of liquid coins exceeds 40% | Simulations only; documented in the docstring of `src/simulate.py` |
| D14 | Table 6: the residual `Res.lnA` is taken on `ln VOLUME` | The residual is taken on `ln A_C` (the same one as in Table 4); in Table 6 it sits next to `ln DVol` | One residual for all tables; `ln A_C` is almost exactly `−ln DVol` (mean cross-sectional correlation in `report_diagnostics.csv`, `corrLnACVol`) | The Table 6 rows in `paper_vs_us` are an approximation; the residual on `ln VOLUME` is listed under Next steps |
| D15 | Task §6.3 (not the paper): value weighting by market cap and dropping the bottom 20% by market cap | DVol-weighted sorts and a "liquid half" (DVol at or above the monthly median; `src/analysis.py`, block `R_weighting`) | No market cap (D2) | The micro-cap check is by volume only, not by size |
| D16 | The paper needs no minimum number of stocks per cross-sectional regression (≈1,775 per month) | FM skips a month with fewer than k+10 coins (k = number of regressors), `src/fama_macbeth.py` (`min_obs`); hard-coded in commit `8b60380`, before the first run, but not moved into `config.yaml` | The early 2019 months with only a handful of coins give degenerate slopes | Our FM is a few early months shorter than the sorts (`sample.csv` vs `T4_fm.csv`); the A&N upgrade verdict depends on this minimum (`amihud_noh/sensitivity_min_obs_counts.csv`) |

Specification changes made after the first real-data run are listed below, with date and reason.

## Change log after the first run

### J1 — 2026-10-16 — Binance data audit (redenominations, ticker reuse)

**What.** Before the measures are computed, the daily klines go through the audit in `src/data_audit.py` (rules and thresholds in a new `audit:` section of `config.yaml`; no pre-registered value was changed). The events of each run are written to `results/<run_id>/audit_events.csv` and `audit_summary.csv`, and the rules to `run_manifest.json` (`audit`). `daily.parquet` was rebuilt from the same local zip files (`data_binance.py --rebuild`, no new download) and now also stores open/high/low, base-currency volume (`volume`) and `taker_buy_quote`. Whether the shared columns are unchanged against the previous file was checked by hand; that check was not saved, so it is not evidence in the repository.

| Event type | Signature | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| `redenomination` | after a trading halt, open = previous close / 10^k (±26%), base-currency volume changes by about 10^k (±1 order of magnitude), dollar volume is continuous (±1 order of magnitude) | as in CRSP: all prices before the event are divided by the factor and base-currency volume is multiplied by it; daily and monthly returns and A are continuous |
| `ticker_reuse`, `long_gap` | a pause of ≥ 14 days (with or without a price jump of ≥ 8×) | from the event date on, a new asset `<SYMBOL>#2`; neither the return nor the beta/momentum/IVOL windows span the pause |
| `discontinuity` | a jump of ≥ 8× across a halt that is not confirmed by volume | a new asset (not spliced) |
| `zero_volume`, `broken_print` | no trades (Binance repeats the last price); price ≤ 0 or close outside [low, high] | the row is dropped |
| `open_mismatch` | open ≠ previous close without a halt (end of pre-market) | reported only |

Key observation: Binance trades 24/7, so without a halt the open of day d is (almost) the close of day d−1: the median |open − previous close| is 3.3 bp and the two are exactly equal on 35% of days (`results/upgrades/edge_spread/edge_describe.csv`, columns `median_abs_open_gap_bp` and `share_open_eq_prev_close`). A real price move therefore shows up within the day, not as a gap at the open. This also holds for the LUNA crash of 11–13 May 2022: it stays in the data as a real −99.99% month (`ret_extremes.csv` of the base run). All redenominations the audit found (BNX, BTCST, COCOS, DREP, QUICK, STRAX, SUN, VIDT) have dates that coincide with trading halts in the klines, and factors that match the shape of the jump in price and in base-currency volume (`audit_events.csv`, evidence column). No project or exchange announcements are stored in the repository, so we do not claim that these events were confirmed against announcements; the evidence is the halt timing and the jump shapes in `audit_events.csv`. LUNA from 31 May 2022 is Terra 2.0, a new asset. The 17 audit events were reviewed by hand; that review was not saved and there is no list of rejected candidates, so the audit's false-positive rate is not documented. The only `open_mismatch` (SCR, 22 Oct 2024) is Binance's switch from pre-market to spot trading and affects nothing.

**Why.** Binance klines are not adjusted for splits and redenominations. In the first run (`results/20261016T134056Z_18099336`, kept in the repository) one uncorrected COCOS redenomination (1000:1 on 23 Jan 2021) gave a monthly return of about +170,916% and drove the mean of a whole quintile; through CMKT it also distorted the betas. The exact figure is not in any CSV of that run: we recomputed it from the pre-audit daily cache (`data/cache/daily_v1_20261016.parquet`, not in git; last close 0.000246 in Dec 2020 and 0.4207 in Jan 2021). Its effect is visible in the CSVs of the first run: the mean coin return in 2021-01 is 1,104.7% across 168 coins (`panel_summary.csv`), and the 2021-01 mean of the A_C quintile Q5 is 5,078% (`T3_portfolios.csv`).

**Effect.** Only monthly returns and everything built on them are affected: ret, ret_adj, CMKT, CMOM, betas, Ret[−1], Ret[−12,−2]. The measures A and A_C did not change, because a daily return across a gap is not computed anyway (D9). Quintiles, H–L and the FM slopes returned to a plausible scale (compare `T3_sorts.csv` and `T4_fm.csv` of the two runs). The audit also changed the inference: in the pre-registered first run no T4 slope reached |t| = 1.96, while after the audit the Res.lnA slopes of models (3)–(5) pass 1.96. The audit thresholds (minimum factor, the 14-day gap rule, the ±1 order-of-magnitude volume bands) were chosen after the first run had been seen, so the Res.lnA significance depends on a data fix made after the first run. The return distribution and the most extreme coin-months are in `ret_distribution.csv`, `ret_extremes.csv` and `ret_influence.csv` of the new run.

### J2 — 2026-10-16 — robustness check "winsorize_returns"

**What.** The pre-registered robustness check `returns.winsorize_returns` is implemented as the block `R_winsor_returns` in `analysis.run_tests`: the T3 sorts (A, A_C, Res.A; raw return and alpha) and the T4 FM (models 1–5) on the returns ret and ret_adj, winsorised at 1/99 within each month. The quantiles are set by a new key `returns.winsorize_returns_q`. The CMKT and CMOM factors are not winsorised. The main specification, as in the paper, has no winsorisation (`winsorize_returns: false`).

**Why.** In crypto, single coin-months with returns of +500% to +1,000% are real (the 2021 alt season, pumps before delisting), not data errors. We need to show that the conclusion does not rest on a few observations.

**Effect.** The main tables do not change; this is an additional table, `R_winsor_returns.csv`.
