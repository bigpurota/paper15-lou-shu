## Thesis (lecture template)

> **The authors claim** that the Amihud measure is priced not because it measures price impact but because it consists almost entirely of inverse dollar volume, **because** the “constant” measure `A_C = mean_d(1/DVol_d)` earns the same premium as `A`, while the part of `A` orthogonal to `A_C` (`Res.A`) is unpriced or priced with a negative sign, **using** quintile sorts and Fama–MacBeth regressions with the signal from month t−2 and Newey–West t-statistics on NYSE/AMEX stocks, 1964–2012. **The weakest step** is the move from “the residual is not priced” to “price impact is not priced”: the decomposition `ln A = ln|Ret| + ln A_C` drops the covariance term `cov(|r|, 1/DVol)` (Amihud & Noh 2021), and in our data the residual is mostly `ln|Ret|` (R² <!--v:an.r2_res_abs-->0.61<!--/v-->), so it may capture volatility rather than price impact (we measured this on Binance only, not on the paper's data). **I would test it by** computing the missing term `DIF` and checking whether it is priced beyond `A_C`; checking whether `Res.lnA` survives controls for `ln|Ret|` and IVOL; and comparing `A` with a direct price-impact λ from 5-minute bars, signed with the bars' aggregate taker-buy volume (no Lee–Ready). The bar λ correlates with a trade-level λ from `aggTrades` (corr of ln λ <!--v:hf.val.corr-->0.86<!--/v-->, Spearman <!--v:hf.val.spearman-->0.80<!--/v-->, <!--v:hf.val.n-->69<!--/v--> coin-months in <!--v:hf.val.n_months-->2<!--/v--> months; its level is about <!--v:hf.val.ratio-->8.1<!--/v--> times higher).

### Our answer in one paragraph

On Binance (<!--v:n.months-->93<!--/v--> months, on average <!--v:n.avgcoins-->265<!--/v--> coins per month) **<!--v:sc.samesign-->25<!--/v--> of <!--v:sc.ls_n-->28<!--/v--> coefficients have the paper's sign**, which is weak evidence because the rows overlap heavily; **the paper's significance repeats, at 1.96 and never at the HLZ bar, mainly for `Res.lnA`** and otherwise only for <!--v:sc.rep196_other_rows-->FM ln|Ret|, with ln A_C (Table 2 model 3) (t -2.50); FM ln A_C (2), first half vs 1964-1988 (t 2.59)<!--/v-->; none of the <!--v:sc.vol_n-->11<!--/v--> full-sample rows of the volume part (`A`, `A_C`) is significant (<!--v:sc.vol_sig-->0<!--/v--> of <!--v:sc.vol_n-->11<!--/v-->). The quintile spread on `A_C` is <!--v:pvu.HLACrawCoef-->1.70 (t 1.12)<!--/v--> %/mo against <!--v:pvu.HLACrawCoef.paper-->0.61 (t 2.95)<!--/v--> for the paper, and the FM slope on `ln A_C` is <!--v:pvu.FMlnAC-->0.760 (t 1.53)<!--/v--> against <!--v:pvu.FMlnAC.paper-->0.183 (t 4.79)<!--/v-->. <!--v:sc.hlz-->0<!--/v--> of the <!--v:sc.ls_n-->28<!--/v--> coefficients in the base table pass the Harvey–Liu–Zhu bar (|t| > 3). This is **not a refutation**: the minimum detectable effect (80% power) for the `A_C` spread is <!--v:mde.hlAC-->4.27<!--/v--> %/mo, <!--v:mde.hlAC.ratio-->7.0<!--/v--> times the paper's premium. Where the paper's `Res.lnA` is significant, our `Res.lnA` repeats that in <!--v:sc.res_rep196-->6<!--/v--> of <!--v:sc.res_paper_sig-->7<!--/v--> rows at 1.96 and in <!--v:sc.res_rep_w0-->5<!--/v--> of <!--v:sc.res_paper_sig-->7<!--/v--> at the world-0 empirical threshold (<!--v:w0.crit_res_min-->2.15<!--/v-->–<!--v:w0.crit_res_max-->2.16<!--/v-->; world 0 calibrated it only for the full-sample models (3) and (4), and <!--v:sc.res_cal_w0-->2<!--/v--> of those <!--v:sc.res_cal_n-->2<!--/v--> rows pass it, but the cut-off is itself uncertain: its 95% CI is <!--v:w0.crit_res_ci-->2.04–2.96<!--/v-->, and <!--v:sc.res_cal_above_ci-->0<!--/v--> of those rows clear its upper end; for the half-sample, Table 6 and raw-return rows it is an approximation). In model (5) the residual is <!--v:an.V3m-->-6.33 (t -1.98)<!--/v-->, and it disappears when `ln|Ret|` is added (<!--v:an.V5m-->-0.143 (t -0.05)<!--/v-->). This is almost mechanical: `ln A = ln|Ret| + ln A_C + DIF`, so once `ln A_C` is removed, the residual is mostly `ln|Ret|` (R² <!--v:an.r2_res_abs-->0.61<!--/v-->) and almost entirely `ln|Ret| + DIF` (R² <!--v:an.r2_res_dif-->0.87<!--/v-->). Such a test cannot separate volatility from price impact. After `|Ret|`, what remains of the residual is the Amihud & Noh term `DIF`, and `DIF` is insignificant (<!--v:an.V1m-->-1.64 (t -0.44)<!--/v--> on monthly measures; <!--v:an.V1y-->14.11 (t 1.55)<!--/v--> on 12-month measures, A&N's sign), and that does **not** show that `DIF` is unpriced: our coefficients are on a different scale from the papers' (|ours / paper| from <!--v:an.scale_min-->1.6<!--/v--> to <!--v:an.scale_max-->36<!--/v--> in the key-regression rows of `amihud_noh/SUMMARY.md`, e.g. <!--v:an.scale_eg-->ln A_C 0.814 vs 0.165; ln|Ret| -9.05 vs -0.319<!--/v-->), so the MDE80 of <!--v:pvu.ANDIF.mde-->10.53<!--/v--> in our units cannot be set against A&N's coefficient <!--v:an.p.col3-->0.996 (t 3.75)<!--/v-->, and our power against an A&N-sized effect is unknown. “The residual is volatility” is a hypothesis for us, not evidence against price impact. Our data **do not settle** Lou & Shu versus Amihud & Noh: A&N's premise holds (`COV < 0` in <!--v:an.covneg-->98.8%<!--/v--> of coin-months); the only result in A&N's direction in the main specification is `fDIF` on 12-month measures (<!--v:an.V4y-->45.12 (t 2.14)<!--/v--> against <!--v:an.p.fdif-->1.261 (t 3.29)<!--/v--> for A&N, below HLZ; on raw returns the monthly `DIF` of V2 also passes 1.96, <!--v:an.ret.V2m-->5.43 (t 2.03)<!--/v-->), and the verdict of our rule (not pre-registered: the rule was written together with its results) changes with the minimum number of coins per month (a post hoc sensitivity check). A direct price impact λ (5-minute bars, sign from taker volume, <!--v:hf.months-->36<!--/v--> months) correlates with `A` at <!--v:hf.corr.A-->0.615<!--/v--> (paper <!--v:hf.corr.A.paper-->0.737<!--/v-->) and has a **negative** FM slope (<!--v:hf.b1-->-2.59 (t -4.03)<!--/v-->). That diverges from model (1) of the paper's Table 9 (<!--v:hf.b1.paper-->-0.034 (t -0.67)<!--/v-->) and from its Table 10; in model (2) the signs of ln λ and ln A_C agree with the paper (<!--v:hf.b2lam.paper-->-0.155 (t -2.51)<!--/v--> and <!--v:hf.b2ac.paper-->0.18 (t 3.80)<!--/v--> for the paper; ours <!--v:hf.b2lam-->-4.30 (t -3.57)<!--/v--> and <!--v:hf.b2ac-->1.06 (t 1.97)<!--/v-->). The window is a single one with <!--v:hf.months-->36<!--/v--> cross-sections, the premium on `A_C` is absent in it (`ln A_C` without λ: <!--v:hf.b0-->-0.435 (t -1.56)<!--/v-->, a negative and insignificant point estimate), so the λ result may be a window effect; we do not reproduce Table 9. On MOEX the positive `ln A_C` slope repeats at 1.96 (<!--v:mx.fm2-->0.308 (t 2.87)<!--/v-->, model (2); HLZ not passed; on raw returns model (2) gives <!--v:mx.raw.fm2ac-->0.114 (t 1.29)<!--/v--> and model (4) <!--v:mx.raw.fm4ac-->0.100 (t 1.15)<!--/v-->, and over the months of the main FM, post hoc, model (4) gives <!--v:mx.rawsame.fm4ac-->0.167 (t 1.97)<!--/v-->), but the paper's negative `Res.lnA` does not (<!--v:mx.fm4res-->-0.038 (t -0.13)<!--/v-->). In the US after 2012 the raw Illiquidity long–short fell (<!--v:op.ill.pre-->0.324 (t 2.78)<!--/v--> → <!--v:op.ill.post-->-0.142 (t -1.47)<!--/v-->); its FF3+UMD alpha is insignificant in both periods (<!--v:op.pre.ff4-->0.136 (t 1.60)<!--/v--> → <!--v:op.post.ff4-->0.165 (t 1.66)<!--/v-->) and its SMB loading fell (<!--v:op.pre.smb-->0.705 (t 14.93)<!--/v--> → <!--v:op.post.smb-->0.241 (t 4.46)<!--/v-->), which is consistent with the drop coming from lower size exposure; we did not test this formally.

## Data

- **Market:** Binance spot, USDT pairs from the `data.binance.vision` listing, daily klines. The listing includes pairs that were later delisted, so they are in the universe; we did not check that the archive holds every pair that ever traded, the number of delisted pairs is not recorded in `results/`, and there are no delisting returns (D10), so a survivorship/delisting bias may remain. Excluded: stablecoins, fiat, gold, wrapped and staking tokens, leveraged tokens (`config.yaml → universe`).
- **Sample:** returns <!--v:n.first-->2019-01<!--/v-->…<!--v:n.last-->2026-09<!--/v-->, <!--v:n.months-->93<!--/v--> months, <!--v:n.coinmonths-->24 628<!--/v--> coin-months, <!--v:n.coins-->626<!--/v--> coins, on average <!--v:n.avgcoins-->265<!--/v--> coins per month; Fama–MacBeth uses <!--v:n.fmmonths-->87<!--/v--> months and on average <!--v:n.fmobs-->214<!--/v--> coins per estimation month (`T4_fm.csv`, `avg_obs` of model (1)). The column `sample.csv → avg_coins_fm` (<!--v:n.avgcoins_fm_all-->201<!--/v-->) averages over all <!--v:n.months-->93<!--/v--> months, including <!--v:n.fm_dropped-->6<!--/v--> early months in which there are fewer coins than the FM minimum (k+10, D16) and no cross-sectional regression is run. Filters as in the paper: at least 10 valid days per month, plus a minimum average dollar volume.
- **Data audit** (`src/data_audit.py`, journal entry J1 in `DEVIATIONS.md`). Binance klines are not adjusted for redenominations. Because Binance trades 24/7, a genuine price move opens at the previous close, whereas a redenomination opens after a trading halt at the close divided by 10^k, with a jump in base-currency volume and continuous dollar volume. The audit found <!--v:audit.TOTAL-->17<!--/v--> events: <!--v:audit.redenomination-->8<!--/v--> redenominations (adjusted like CRSP splits), <!--v:audit.ticker_reuse-->2<!--/v--> ticker reuses and <!--v:audit.long_gap-->4<!--/v--> long gaps (new asset `<SYM>#2`), <!--v:audit.zero_volume-->2<!--/v--> zero-volume runs (rows dropped), <!--v:audit.open_mismatch-->1<!--/v--> case reported only. Daily rows: <!--v:audit.rows_in-->788 692<!--/v--> → <!--v:audit.rows_out-->788 689<!--/v-->; asset identifiers: <!--v:audit.ids_in-->678<!--/v--> → <!--v:audit.ids_out-->684<!--/v-->. The dates of all redenominations coincide with trading halts in the klines, and the factors match the shape of the price and base-volume jumps (`audit_events.csv`). That is the only evidence in the repository: no project or exchange announcements are stored, so we do not claim that the events were confirmed against announcements (`DEVIATIONS.md`, J1). The LUNA crash of May 2022 stays in the data as a real decline.

## How to run

Requires `Python 3.12` (`make venv` uses `uv` if it is installed, otherwise `python3.12 -m venv` and `pip`) and network access on the first run. For `make slides`: TeX Live (`xelatex`, `latexmk`) and the Arial font. `make all` has been run end to end on a fresh clone of the final code (<!--v:fc.rc-->exit code 0<!--/v-->, about <!--v:fc.min-->9<!--/v--> min, with `data/` <!--v:fc.data-->a directory of the clone (a copy of the cached downloads)<!--/v-->), and a fresh clone of an earlier version of the code with an empty `data/` redid every download (`<!--v:repro.steps-->venv → data → tests → p0 → upgrades-light → upgrade-hf_lambda (3 attempts)<!--/v-->`, <!--v:repro.rc-->every step exited with code 0, although 2 of the `upgrade-hf_lambda` attempts had failed downloads<!--/v-->); see “Reproducibility”. ```bash
make venv       # .venv + pinned versions from requirements.txt (uv, or venv + pip)
make p0         # data → tests → sims → run → report → figures   (steps 6–8 of the course scale)

#### Key findings
- **Scorecard.** Of 28 Lou & Shu coefficient rows, 13 match, 14 match in sign only and 1 is 'no' under the rule below the table; by sign alone, 25 of 28 have the paper's sign (2 'yes' rows have the opposite sign but are insignificant in both samples, which the rule counts as a match); 10 of these t-statistics have |t| ≥ 1.96 and 0 have |t| > 3 (HLZ bar). Several rows are overlapping specifications of the same few signals, so they are not independent tests. Against Amihud & Noh (2021), 2 of 2 rows have the opposite sign (A&N col. 4 is the same regression as our model (5)). 31 of 45 robustness variants keep the base conclusion (`robustness_vs_base.csv`; same sign and same side of 1.96; the 7 variants without the most influential month were added after the first real-data run and are labelled post hoc): 24 of them are insignificant estimates that stay insignificant, and 7 of the 8 variants of significant base estimates stay significant.
- **Volume component (A_C).** Sorts give 1.701 (t 1.12) and FM gives 0.760 (t 1.53); both have the paper's sign, and they are not significant. The most influential month for the sort is 2021-03 (H-L 150.3%, CMKT 139.4%); without it the spread is 0.09 (t 0.08). The 80%-power MDE for the A_C spread is 4.27%/mo against the paper's 0.61, so our insignificant spreads cannot reject a stock-sized premium.
- **Different market.** Across coins volatility and dollar volume move together (corr(ln DVol, ln|Ret|) = 0.26, slope 1.12), and corr(A, |Ret|) is -0.133, against 0.489 for stocks.

**Match rule.** Coefficients and spreads: if the paper is significant (|t| ≥ 1.96), *yes* = same sign and |t| ≥ 1.96, *sign only* = same sign but not significant, *no* = opposite sign. If the paper is not significant, *yes* = we are not significant either, *sign only* / *no* = we are significant with the same / opposite sign. Levels (correlations, Q75/Q25): *yes* = same sign and within half an order of magnitude (|log10(us/paper)| ≤ 0.5), *sign only* = same sign otherwise; Q75/Q25 ratios are positive by construction, so they get *yes* or *no* on magnitude alone. *Months needed* (in the A_C sort row) assumes the SE falls as 1/sqrt(T): an estimate. Units: H-L and alphas in %/month; FM slopes in %/month per unit of the regressor (paper: FF3-adjusted; us: CMKT-adjusted, D4). Deviations D1–D18, upgrade deviations U1–U8 and journal entries J1–J2: `DEVIATIONS.md`.

## Deviations from the paper

The full list with reasons and expected effects is in [`DEVIATIONS.md`](DEVIATIONS.md). The main ones:

- **D1–D3.** A different market and period (Binance instead of NYSE/AMEX, a short sample); no token supply, hence no `AT`, `AT_C`, size or `B/M` (turnover measures exist only on MOEX, upgrade 6).
- **D4, D6, D7.** CMKT-adjusted returns instead of FF3-adjusted ones (the EW market of the universe, 36-month betas); alphas on CMKT+CMOM instead of four factors; IVOL is the residual of a daily one-factor model.
- **D9, D10.** Close-to-close returns on UTC days; at delisting the return runs to the last close (Binance has no delisting returns).
- **D11.** The paper's January test checks whether the volume premium shares the January seasonality of the liquidity premium (the paper's section “Tests of the Liquidity Explanation”); with only <!--v:t7.jan.n-->7<!--/v--> Januaries our version has very little power, so for us it is a placebo.
- **D14–D17.** The Table 6 residual is taken on `ln A_C`, not on `ln VOLUME`; instead of value weights and dropping micro-caps, DVol weights and a “liquid half”; the minimum number of coins per month for FM (k+10) is hard-coded in `src/fama_macbeth.py` (the A&N verdict depends on it); the Table 2 models with `ln AT_C` and `ln S` and the turnover version of Table 7 exist only on MOEX.
- **J1, J2** (after the first run): the redenomination audit; a robustness variant with winsorized returns. No pre-registered value was changed, but J1 changed the results: in the pre-registered first run no T4 slope reached |t| = 1.96 (Res.lnA in model (3): <!--v:fr.fm3res-->-128.81 (t -1.06)<!--/v-->), and the audit rules were designed after that run (see “Overfitting”).
- **U1–U8** are the upgrades' deviations: λ from 5-minute bars with taker volume, on trade prices, over a short window; daily EDGE instead of hourly; Fear & Greed instead of Baker–Wurgler and AR(2) of the EW `A` instead of Pástor–Stambaugh; OpenAP portfolios and French factors instead of our own CRSP sorts; A&N 12-month measures next to the monthly ones; MOEX in `results/upgrades/moex/deviations.csv`.

## Simulations: can the method tell the hypotheses apart?

Before the first real-data estimation run (the klines were already downloading), the pipeline was run on three synthetic worlds (`src/simulate.py`, <!--v:sim.assets-->300<!--/v--> coins × <!--v:sim.months-->90<!--/v--> months, daily data): **world V**, where expected returns depend only on volume; **world P**, only on price impact beyond the square-root law; **world 0**, no premium. In world V `A_C` is priced (<!--v:sim.V.fm2-->100%<!--/v--> of runs with t > 1.96, mean t <!--v:sim.V.fm2.t-->7.2<!--/v-->) and `Res.lnA` is not (<!--v:sim.V.fm3-->4%<!--/v-->); in world P `Res.lnA` is priced (FM: <!--v:sim.P.fm3-->100%<!--/v-->, mean t <!--v:sim.P.fm3.t-->4.8<!--/v-->; the quintile sort on `Res.A` only <!--v:sim.P.hlResA-->76%<!--/v-->), but `ln A_C` is also falsely significant in <!--v:sim.P.fm2-->18%<!--/v--> of world-P runs, so a significant `A_C` alone does not single out world V. ## Overfitting: how the results could be overfitted and how we checked

<!-- BEGIN:overfitting -->
- **Pre-registration.** The specification (`config.yaml`, `DEVIATIONS.md` D1–D11) was fixed before the first real-data run; the refinements of `DEVIATIONS.md` (D7 rewritten, D12 and D13 added) were also made before it. A copy of the pre-registered `config.yaml` is `docs/preregistration/config_preregistered.yaml` (sha256 `18099336…`); the manifest of the first real-data run (`results/20261016T134056Z_18099336/run_manifest.json`) records config sha256 `18099336…`, which matches that file, so the first run used the pre-registered specification (`make verify` rechecks this). That the specification was fixed before the data were seen (the time order) rests on our version history, kept in the public repository; it cannot be checked from the files of the ZIP alone. Changes after the first run are in the journal (J1 data audit, J2 winsorization), each with a reason. J1 matters for the results: in the pre-registered first run, which still had the unadjusted COCOS redenomination, no FM slope in T4 reached |t| = 1.96 (largest |t| 1.79; Res.lnA in model (3) -128.81 (t -1.06), `20261016T134056Z_18099336/T4_fm.csv`). The audit rules (thresholds and the gap rule) were designed after that run, so the Res.lnA significance depends on a data fix made after the first run.
- **Test ledger** (`results/upgrades/robustness/ledger_summary.csv`). Base family: 73 unique tests, |t| > 1.96 in 16 (with all effects zero we would expect 3.65 at the nominal 5% and about 5.7 at the world-0 size of NW(6)), |t| > 3 in 0; after Bonferroni (|t| threshold 3.40) 0, after BH 0. Base and upgrades together: 750 unique tests (1020 ledger rows with role “test”), |t| > 3 in 66 (by source: base 0, amihud_noh 1, hf_lambda 25, moex 29, openap_us 8, sentiment 3), Bonferroni (|t| threshold 3.99) 14, BH 90. These counts overstate the number of distinct findings: the ledger also labels 18 factor-loading rows, the ln ME control and identical estimates under different NW lags as tests. By kind, the Bonferroni survivors are: hf_lambda: ln λ 5, T10 cost part 2, lnA_C 1; moex: factor loading 5; sentiment: Long-Short 1. Of these, 5 are MOEX factor loadings (not hypothesis tests); the 8 hf_lambda rows all come from the one 36-month λ window (T10 cost part (main, lambda, model (1)); T10 cost part (main, lambda+EDGE, model (1)); ln λ (main (same as T9B), model (1)); lnA_C (first half (2023-10..2025-03), model (2)); ln λ (second half (2025-04..2026-09), model (1)); ln λ (second half (2025-04..2026-09), model (2)); ln λ (ret_adj winsorized 1/99 within month, model (1)); ln λ (ret_adj winsorized 1/99 within month, model (2))); the 1 sentiment row(s): illiq_ar2 with controls CMKT, Long-Short (the main market-illiquidity specification uses controls CMKT+CMOM).
- **Test size.** In world 0 (300 coins × 90 months: a T similar to ours, but a larger, balanced cross-section with lighter-tailed noise), NW(6) gives false |t| > 1.96 in 6.5%–9.5% of runs (|t| > 3 in at most 1.5%): all point estimates are above 5%, but only the Res.lnA rates have a 95% CI that excludes 5% at 200 runs. The empirical 5% critical |t| is 2.00–2.22 (for Res.lnA 2.15–2.16, 95% CI 2.04–2.96). Simulation criteria passed: 14 of 17; failed: world P, hl_ResA: 76.0% (rule ≥ 80%); world 0, fm3_ResLnA: 9.5% (95% CI 5.8%–14.4% excludes 5%); world 0, fm4_ResLnA: 9.5% (95% CI 5.8%–14.4% excludes 5%). 5 of the passed criteria use the loose rule “share ≤ 20%” for signals that should be unpriced (world P falsely prices ln A_C in 18% of runs); with a ≤ 5% rule for those, 11 of 17 would pass. The P0 item “about 5% in world 0” is therefore not met, which is why we show the world-0 empirical threshold and the HLZ bar of 3 next to 1.96. This calibration covers the base sample length only (not the 36-month λ window or the overlapping 12-month A&N measures), and its balanced, lighter-tailed panel is not our real panel.
- **Block bootstrap over months** (6-month blocks, 2000 draws, plus 20 seeds): the CI excludes zero for 3 of 11 statistics, robustly across seeds for 2 (FM Res.lnA (3) [-8.98; -1.52], (4) [-15.29; -2.45]); FM ln A_C (4) is borderline (11/20 seeds exclude zero).
- **Placebo** (`placebo.csv`): shuffling the measures within a month (20 permutations, so the shares are coarse): mean t across permutations at most 0.32 in absolute value, s.d. of t across permutations 0.77–1.12, share of |t| > 1.96 at most 10%. A leak placebo (lag 0 instead of t−2) gives |t| 2.58–6.02. Lag 0 is a contemporaneous measure, so part of that |t| is the mechanical return–volume link; the future-lag (t+1) placebo of task §13.1 was not run.
- **Stability.** Without 2021, H-L A_C 0.432 (t 0.42), FM ln A_C 0.351 (t 0.97), Res.lnA (3) -4.93 (t -2.54); leaving out one year, the t of H-L A_C ranges over 0.42..1.32. Winsorized returns, raw returns, weighting, sub-periods and without the most influential month: 31 of 45 variants keep the base conclusion (`robustness_vs_base.csv`; same sign and same side of 1.96), including 7 post hoc variants without the most influential month (chosen from the data and added after the first real-data run). 24 of these 31 are insignificant estimates that stay insignificant; 7 of the 8 variants of the significant statistics (FM Res.lnA (3), FM Res.lnA (4)) stay significant.
- **Power.** MDE80 for H-L A_C is 4.27%/mo against the paper's premium of 0.61. For FM ln A_C: 1.39 per unit of ln A_C against the paper's slope 0.183 (7.6× larger); per 1 s.d., 2.15 (our s.d. 1.54) against 0.46 for the paper (its s.d. 2.53, pp. 13–14), i.e. 4.6×, or 0.28 if the paper's slope is multiplied by our s.d. The real-data MDE is roughly 5× the simulation MDE (an estimate: the real Newey–West-based MDE against a simulation MDE rescaled to our sample size from a power curve with 20 replications per point), so the simulations understate the noise. Insignificance here does not show that a premium of the paper's size is absent.
- **Survivorship and look-ahead.** The universe includes USDT pairs that were later delisted (they are in the `data.binance.vision` listing); whether that archive holds every pair ever traded was not checked, the number of delisted pairs is not recorded in `results/`, and delisting returns are missing (D10). ## Known limitations

- **Power.** <!--v:n.months-->93<!--/v--> months against <!--v:d.paperMonths-->588<!--/v--> in the paper and very noisy coin returns: insignificant results do not prove that a premium is absent (MDE80 is higher; see “Overfitting”).
- **One month.** The `A_C` spread rests on the altcoin season of March 2021: without it, <!--v:d.exMeanAC-->0.09<!--/v--> %/mo (t <!--v:d.exTAC-->0.08<!--/v-->).
- **No size.** Without market caps there is no `ln(ME)` control. On MOEX, where size exists, the insignificant sort spread loads on SMB (<!--v:mx.smb-->0.906 (t 8.54)<!--/v-->), but FM with an `ln ME` control keeps `ln A_C` (<!--v:mx.fm4ac-->0.298 (t 2.85)<!--/v-->; MOEX `ME` uses today's share count for every past month, a look-ahead in the size control); whether either carries over to crypto was not tested.
- **Opposite-sign correlations.** corr(A, |Ret|) is <!--v:pvu.corrAabs-->-0.133<!--/v--> for us against <!--v:pvu.corrAabs.paper-->0.489<!--/v--> for the paper: in crypto, dollar volume rises with volatility.
- **λ.** Our λ comes from 5-minute bars (trade prices, not midquotes; sign from the bars' aggregate taker volume) over <!--v:hf.months-->36<!--/v--> months in which the `A_C` premium is also absent; the negative λ diverges from model (1) of Table 9 instead of reproducing it.
- **EDGE.** On daily bars of a 24/7 market, EDGE is almost entirely the open − previous close gap (weight <!--v:ed.w2-->0.9996<!--/v-->), so it does not measure the spread. The break-even cost of <!--v:ed.breakeven-->1.52%<!--/v--> per side is computed from a gross spread that is an insignificant point estimate (<!--v:ed.gross-->1.65 (t 1.07)<!--/v-->) and rests on March 2021 (without that month the `A_C` spread is <!--v:d.exMeanAC-->0.09<!--/v--> %/mo, which leaves almost no room for costs).
