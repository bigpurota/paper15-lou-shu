### 2. Problem: the Amihud measure is priced, but for what?
- **Speaker:** Marat Bineman.
- **Message:** assets that are illiquid by the Amihud measure earn a premium; it is usually read as compensation for price impact. The paper asks whether that reading is right.
- **Show:** the formula and one idea: "return per unit of volume".
- **Formula:** `A = mean_d(|r_d| / DVol_d)`.
- **Key numbers:** the paper's premium: quintile H-L on A <!--v:pvu.HLArawCoef.paper-->0.56 (t 2.36)<!--/v--> %/month (NYSE/AMEX 1964–2012, <!--v:d.paperMonths-->588<!--/v--> months).
- **Notes:** Amihud (2002) is the most widely used illiquidity proxy. If the premium is for price impact, the measure serves both to measure liquidity and to explain returns; Lou & Shu argue these are two different questions. Thesis in one sentence: the authors claim that the Amihud premium is a trading-volume premium rather than a price-impact premium, because its volume component A_C carries the premium while the residual does not, using sorts and Fama–MacBeth regressions on NYSE/AMEX 1964–2012; the weakest step is reading the residual as "price impact" when it is mostly |Ret|; we test it with the Amihud & Noh decomposition and a direct λ benchmark.
- **Literature (one line on the slide):** the measure (Amihud 2002) → validation against high-frequency benchmarks (Goyenko–Holden–Trzcinka 2009) → what is priced (Lou & Shu 2017) → critique of the decomposition (Amihud & Noh 2021) → better spread estimates from OHLC (EDGE, Ardia–Guidotti–Kroencke 2024). There is no separate literature backup slide; the details are in the Q&A notes (ROLES.md).
- **Time:** 1 min.

- **Key numbers:** corr(A, A_C): paper <!--v:pvu.corrAAC.paper-->0.899<!--/v-->, us <!--v:pvu.corrAAC-->0.956<!--/v-->. Q75/Q25 of A_C: paper <!--v:pvu.QratioAC.paper-->116.60<!--/v-->, us <!--v:pvu.QratioAC-->9.40<!--/v-->; of |Ret|: paper <!--v:pvu.QratioAbs.paper-->2.18<!--/v-->, us <!--v:pvu.QratioAbs-->1.75<!--/v-->. corr(A, |Ret|): paper <!--v:pvu.corrAabs.paper-->0.489<!--/v-->, us <!--v:pvu.corrAabs-->-0.133<!--/v-->.
- **Notes:** our dispersion of liquidity is an order of magnitude smaller than on NYSE/AMEX (the volume filter and Binance's listing policy are a hypothesis for why), but the conclusion is the same: A correlates with A_C at <!--v:pvu.corrAAC-->0.956<!--/v-->. ### 4. Method: a "constant" measure and a residual
- **Speaker:** Sofya Shapovalova.
- **Message:** if A_C is priced and the part of A beyond A_C is not, the Amihud premium is a volume premium.
- **Show:** the scheme "A → A_C (volume) + Res.A (|r| and price impact)"; the two hypotheses and what each predicts: "volume premium" → A_C is priced, "price-impact premium" → Res.A is priced.
- **Formula (one on the slide):** `A_C = mean_d(1 / DVol_d)`; `Res.lnA` is stated in words: the residual of a regression of `ln A` on `ln A_C` within each month (the regression itself is on backup B1).
- **Notes:** the residual is computed in each cross-section, so it is orthogonal to volume by construction. We first tested on simulations whether the pipeline can tell the hypotheses apart (<!--v:sim.checks.pass-->14<!--/v--> of <!--v:sim.checks.n-->17<!--/v--> criteria pass; <!--v:sim.checks.nfail-->3<!--/v--> fail: the world-0 size criterion for Res.lnA, models (3) and (4), and the world-P power of the Res.A sort): in world V, A_C is priced (<!--v:sim.V.fm2-->100%<!--/v--> of simulations); in world P, Res.lnA is priced (FM <!--v:sim.P.fm3-->100%<!--/v-->; the quintile sort on Res.A only <!--v:sim.P.hlResA-->76%<!--/v-->). Two caveats we say out loud: in world P, ln A_C is also falsely significant in <!--v:sim.P.fm2-->18%<!--/v--> of runs, so A_C significance alone does not single out world V; and the simulated noise is much smaller than the real one (the real SE of the A_C spread is about <!--v:d.seRatioHlAC-->3.4<!--/v-->–<!--v:d.seRatioHlACnw-->3.5<!--/v--> times the simulated SE; an estimate that sets our Newey–West SE against the spread's s.d. across world-0 runs or their mean Newey–West SE), so near-perfect discrimination in simulation does not mean the real-data tests can discriminate.
- **Time:** 1 min 30 s.

### 12. Critique

## Likely questions and prepared answers

Owner in brackets; every member must still be able to give the short answer (see ROLES.md, "Everyone must know").

1. **Why lag t−2 and not t−1?** (Marat) The paper only says it follows the literature (Brennan, Huh and Subrahmanyam 2013) and gives no reason. Our reason: to avoid bid–ask bounce and one-month reversal. The lag-0 placebo gives |t| up to <!--v:plc.leak_maxabs_t-->6.02<!--/v-->, but lag 0 is a contemporaneous measure, so this is not a pure leak test. `tests/test_alignment.py` recomputes the lagged signal and controls on a synthetic panel and fails if the lag is 1 or 0; it does not cover Res.lnA, |Ret|, DIF, the 12-month measures or the upgrade panels.
2. **Why adjusted rather than raw returns?** (Marat / Ksenia) To remove systematic risk from the dependent variable (CMKT instead of FF3 for us). On raw returns: ln A_C <!--v:pvu.RawlnAC-->0.860 (t 1.70)<!--/v-->, Res.lnA <!--v:pvu.RawResLnA-->-3.70 (t -2.11)<!--/v-->; note that on raw returns the paper's own Res.lnA is not significant (<!--v:pvu.RawResLnA.paper-->-0.143 (t -1.13)<!--/v-->) and ours is below the world-0 cut-off <!--v:d.critFmThree-->2.15<!--/v-->.
3. **Why logarithms?** (Marat) The measures are heavily skewed; in logs the volume trend and scale go into the monthly intercept, and the slope reads as the effect per unit of log volume.
4. **Why Newey–West with 6 lags?** (Sofya) The monthly FM coefficients are autocorrelated. In world 0 (a similar T, but a larger, balanced cross-section with lighter tails than ours), NW(6) gives <!--v:sim.0.fm3-->9.5%<!--/v--> false |t| > 1.96 for Res.lnA against <!--v:sim.0.fm3nw0-->5.0%<!--/v--> without lags; that rate rejects 5% (a P0 criterion fails), while the other world-0 rates are above 5% without rejecting it, so we also show the empirical cut-off and the threshold 3.
5. **If λ is not priced, does that mean liquidity is not priced?** (Andrey) Not necessarily. The paper validates its λ (comparison with Hasbrouck's estimates, rerun without 2006–2012); Lee–Ready error is a hypothesis we did not test. Our λ (5-minute bars, sign from taker volume) has a negative FM slope (<!--v:hf.b1-->-2.59 (t -4.03)<!--/v-->) in one <!--v:hf.months-->36<!--/v-->-month window, where the size of NW(6) is not calibrated and ln A_C is itself negative, so it may be a window effect; in the paper's model (2) the sign is the same (<!--v:hf.b2lam.paper-->-0.155 (t -2.51)<!--/v-->). Post hoc IVOL and dollar-volume controls do not absorb λ in this window (IVOL: <!--v:hf.ivol.b1-->-2.11 (t -2.93)<!--/v-->; dollar volume: <!--v:hf.vol.b1-->-3.92 (t -3.78)<!--/v-->); there is no true size control for coins (no market caps, D2), so do not claim to have ruled out size; with one short window we do not interpret it as a liquidity discount.
6. **What is the Amihud & Noh critique and what do your data show?** (Andrey) The mean of a ratio is not the ratio of means: the term cov(|r|, 1/DVol) is lost, and it is negative. In crypto this holds (COV < 0 in <!--v:an.covneg-->98.8%<!--/v-->), but DIF is insignificant (<!--v:an.V1y-->14.11 (t 1.55)<!--/v--> on 12-month measures, A&N's sign, against <!--v:an.p.col3-->0.996 (t 3.75)<!--/v--> for A&N; <!--v:an.V1m-->-1.64 (t -0.44)<!--/v--> on monthly measures). Our coefficients are on a different scale from A&N's (|ours / paper| <!--v:an.scale_min-->1.6<!--/v-->–<!--v:an.scale_max-->36<!--/v-->), so we cannot say whether we could detect an A&N-sized effect; we claim only that DIF is insignificant, and the data do not discriminate.
7. **Why is Res.A negative?** (Andrey / Alexia) The residual after volume is mostly |Ret|: corr(Res.lnA, ln|Ret|) <!--v:an.corr_res_absret-->0.78<!--/v-->; with ln|Ret| and IVOL the slope is <!--v:an.V5m-->-0.143 (t -0.05)<!--/v-->. But this is almost an identity (ln A = ln|Ret| + ln A_C + DIF; R² of Res.lnA on ln|Ret| + DIF <!--v:an.r2_res_dif-->0.87<!--/v-->), so the test does not separate volatility from price impact. IVOL alone does not absorb it (<!--v:an.V3m-->-6.33 (t -1.98)<!--/v-->), and the paper also controls IVOL. What remains after |Ret| is DIF, which is insignificant (<!--v:an.V1m-->-1.64 (t -0.44)<!--/v-->). The IVOL-puzzle link is a hypothesis.
8. **Why EW rather than VW?** (Ksenia) Because the paper uses EW. Variants: the liquid half and DVol weights; the A_C spread in the liquid half is <!--v:rv.liq.hlAC-->0.157 (t 0.07)<!--/v-->; all variants in <!--v:base.dir-->results/20261016T221825Z_3e8eb87e<!--/v-->/robustness_vs_base.csv.
